I've seen people talk about spending months or years building a product, only for someone else to make something similar with AI in a few weeks and get more attention. I think I'd be upset too, especially if I'd spent all that time caring about the details and struggling to find users.

But I keep coming back to a question: how did the other person get people to notice?

Maybe they already had an audience or money to spend. Maybe they got lucky. Or maybe they knew how to explain the product in a way that made people want to try it. I don't think more users automatically means a better product, but I'd still want to understand what happened.

I know I tend to value the building part. I care about the code, the design, the performance, and the little decisions that go into making something feel right. Those things matter to me, but I can see how easy it would be to spend all my time on them and assume people will eventually find what I've made.

Someone who knows how to reach people has spent time learning too. I've spent years learning to build software; someone else might have spent those years learning how to explain what they made or figure out who might want it. I don't think my effort is worth more just because I understand it better.

I've also been thinking about how much of what I build comes from things I've already seen. If I make a budgeting app, I might like the way one app shows transactions or how another handles recurring expenses. I'd make my own choices, but I wouldn't be starting from nothing.

Sometimes it goes beyond taking inspiration. Someone can use AI to copy a product's design and features almost exactly. I can see why the person who made the original would be angry. I think I would be too.

Even then, I'd want to understand how they got people to try it. Though it's easy to say that when I'm not the person watching it happen. I might be jealous or angry first. I don't know how quickly I'd move past that.

Lately I've been thinking about what being a solo builder means to me. I used to think mostly about doing all the technical stuff myself: frontend, backend, database, deployment. But if I want to build a business around something, I also need to think about who it's for, how they find it, and why they would keep using it.

I can't keep telling myself I'll figure those things out once the product is finished. There's always something else I could build or improve.

I don't want to stop caring about engineering, and I don't think every developer needs to become a founder. I just want to get better at talking about what I make and listening to the people who might use it. I'm still figuring out what that looks like for me.
